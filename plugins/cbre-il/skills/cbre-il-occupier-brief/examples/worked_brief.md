## The 30-second version
Harrowgate Provisions Group is a GBP 1.84bn revenue foodservice distributor, family-controlled since
1961, supplying 14,200 pubs, care homes and school kitchens from eleven depots and two national
ambient hubs. Trading is healthy and the balance sheet is not: revenue grew 7.4 per cent in the year
to 28 February 2026 while adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 3.9 per cent, and net debt sits at GBP
214m, 2.9 times EBITDA, against facilities that mature in October 2027. In June 2026 the board
announced Project Meridian, a plan to consolidate eleven depots to seven by 2029 and to build a
single 480,000 sq ft chilled national distribution centre in the Midlands. The trigger for us is
narrower and more urgent than the programme: the Wakefield lease, 214,000 sq ft and the second
largest chilled site in the estate, expires on 24 March 2028 with a tenant-only break in March 2027
that has not been exercised. That break date is eleven months away, the Midlands site has no
planning consent, and the refinancing lands between the two. Harrowgate has a sequencing problem
before it has a property problem, and sequencing is the conversation CBRE can lead.

## What is driving the business right now
- **Project Meridian is the whole story.** Announced 12 June 2026: eleven depots to seven by 2029,
  anchored on one 480,000 sq ft chilled NDC in the Midlands, with GBP 30m of annual run-rate savings
  claimed. No site has been named publicly and no application has been validated.
- **Margin recovery is the promise the board has made to its lenders.** Adjusted EBITDA margin fell
  70 basis points in FY26, and management has guided to 4.5 per cent by FY28. Meridian carries almost
  all of that.
- **Care-home exposure cuts both ways.** Care homes are 31 per cent of revenue and the most
  service-sensitive channel in foodservice: a consolidation that lengthens delivery routes threatens
  the revenue that funds it.
- **Chilled capacity is the binding constraint.** Management described chilled capacity as
  "effectively full at peak" at the FY26 results on 21 May 2026, which is why the NDC is chilled
  rather than ambient.
- **The refinancing is the clock nobody mentions in the press release.** GBP 250m of facilities
  mature in October 2027, thirteen months before the NDC is meant to open, and lenders will want the
  property strategy settled before they price it.

## The industry backdrop
- **UK foodservice distribution is consolidating around scale in chilled.** The national players
  have spent five years building single large chilled hubs while regional independents run multi-site
  ambient estates, and Harrowgate is explicitly moving from the second model to the first.
- **Labour, not space, now sets the cost of a depot.** Warehouse and driver pay has risen faster
  than rent in every region Harrowgate operates in, which changes the arithmetic of consolidation:
  the saving comes from fewer shifts, not fewer square feet.
- **Chilled and frozen space is structurally short.** Purpose-built chilled units of 400,000 sq ft
  and above rarely come to the market speculatively, so a requirement of this size is a
  design-and-build or a conversion, both of which carry programme risk that the FY29 date does not
  obviously accommodate.

## Where the next node probably goes
*CBRE analysis, not a Harrowgate disclosure. An inference throughout, and it carries the measured error
bar below. Note what the model is doing here: Harrowgate has already named its cluster, so the question
is not where but whether the announced plan is deliverable and where inside the Midlands it lands.*

**This is a cost-reducing consolidation, not a growth-enabling node, and that changes the urgency.**
Harrowgate's outlet base grows slowly, from 14,200 today to roughly 16,400 by 2029 on its own five-year
rate, and the existing eleven depots demonstrably serve it. Nothing is blocked. What creates the date is
not growth at all: it is the Wakefield break in March 2027 and the refinancing in October 2027. Say that
plainly, because the room will assume the consolidation programme is the clock and it is not.

**The zone.** Weighting the outlet base by drop volume puts the centre of gravity in the Midlands, which
is where the board has said it is going, so the model agrees with the plan. Pulling toward the inbound
gateway moves it very little, because Harrowgate's inbound is domestic production and primary producers,
not a port: for a producer-fed network the node follows the supply base rather than the coast. The
defensible resolution is the triangle between Birmingham, Nottingham and Leicester. Inside a chilled
network the distance threshold collapses to roughly 90 minutes of drive time rather than a driver day, so
a single site more than that from the outlet mass fails on service before it fails on cost.

**The template, from how Harrowgate has consolidated before.** Its last two depot closures went into
existing leased units within twelve months, not into design-and-build. A 480,000 sq ft purpose-built
chilled national distribution centre would be a change of grammar for this business, which is itself a
reason to doubt the announced plan's timetable.

**The window.** Comparable distributors run roughly 2,000 outlets per chilled node, which the forward
outlet base clears comfortably on seven depots. So the trigger is not the constraint: the constraint is
the March 2027 break, eleven months out, against a design-and-build programme that has no consent.

**The error bar, measured.** Run as at 2020 against Harrowgate's own 2021 consolidation, the model picked
the right cluster and placed the site 38 miles from where the business actually went. Treat the zone as
the answer and the town as a guess.

**What would make this wrong.** Harrowgate does not exercise the Wakefield break, renews short, and
defers the whole programme past the refinancing, which is the cheapest thing a stretched board can do.
Or it takes an existing chilled unit and abandons the single-hub design entirely.

**Three things that would confirm it inside ninety days.** A pre-application or screening request lodged
with a Midlands planning authority. A senior supply chain or programme hire with a national
distribution-centre remit. A large chilled unit or a consented site in the triangle going under offer.

## The regulatory and legislative read
Three live items shape where Harrowgate can put the NDC and what the existing estate costs to hold.

- **The 2026 rating revaluation lands mid-programme.** The new list takes effect in April 2026 on
  2024 values, and large distribution warehousing has carried above-average increases. Implication:
  the holding cost of the four depots Meridian closes rises before the savings arrive, which
  strengthens the case for accelerating the exits rather than phasing them.
- **MEES tightens on commercial lettings.** The minimum EPC standard for continued commercial
  letting rises to band C, and three of the ten leased depots are pre-2000 chilled buildings, which
  are the hardest and most expensive class to improve. Implication: on those three sites the
  landlord's improvement obligation and Harrowgate's exit economics are in direct tension, and that
  is a negotiation, not a certainty.
- **Planning for large chilled units is the programme risk.** A 480,000 sq ft chilled NDC carries
  refrigeration plant, standby generation and 24-hour HGV movements, which in most Midlands
  authorities means a full environmental statement and a committee decision rather than delegated
  consent. Implication: on current Midlands determination timescales, an application validated after
  the second quarter of 2027 does not deliver an operating building by FY29.
- **Driver hours and HGV supply remain the quiet constraint.** Enforcement of driver hours and the
  tachograph regime has tightened, and a seven-depot network means longer legs to the same 14,200
  outlets. Implication: route feasibility, not building size, decides whether seven depots can hold
  the service promise, and that is modelling work.

## Why this matters to CBRE
Harrowgate has published a network answer without having done the network work. That is the gap we
walk into, and it is a modelling conversation before it is a property one.

- **Model the seven-depot network before the site is chosen.** SC Navigator and AIMMS can test
  whether seven locations hold the care-home service promise, and which seven, with route and modal
  cost overlays. This is the question their own service constraint makes unavoidable.
- **De-risk the NDC on labour and consent, not on rent.** Labour and Location Analytics sizes the
  warehouse and driver pool at each candidate Midlands location, and our planning and development
  teams can price the programme risk on a chilled scheme of this size honestly.
- **Test automation readiness before the specification freezes.** The CBRE and FORTNA partnership
  assesses clear height, power, floor flatness and throughput envelope, which is what turns their
  GBP 26m automation intent into a specification a developer can build to.
- **Turn four exits and three freeholds into the funding.** Capital Markets and a sale-and-leaseback
  on the freehold estate can fund a material share of the programme ahead of the October 2027
  refinancing, and Lease Advisory handles the Wakefield break and the chilled dilapidations exposure.
