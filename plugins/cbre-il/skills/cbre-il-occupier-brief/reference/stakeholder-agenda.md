# The three persona sections: what is likely on the mind of each stakeholder

Three required sections, **exactly three bullets each**, in this order:

1. `## What is likely on the mind of the Head of Real Estate`
2. `## What is likely on the mind of the Head of Supply Chain`
3. `## What is likely on the mind of the CEO`

The order is deliberate: the two people the I&L team is most likely to be sitting in front of come
first, then the frame their boss has set for them. They sit after the regulatory read and before
"Why this matters to CBRE", because they are the bridge from what is true about the company to what
the person across the table is carrying into the room.

These are the sections a broker actually uses. They are also the easiest place in the whole brief to
fabricate, so the evidence rules below are strict.

## Name the post-holder first

Open each section with a single line, not a bullet, naming the person:

`*Sarah Whitcombe, Property Director since 2023, previously Head of Estates at Booker.*`

If the post-holder is not publicly identifiable, say exactly that, and write to the role:

`*The property lead is not publicly named; written to the role.*`

Do not infer a name from an org chart you have not seen, do not promote somebody's LinkedIn title
into an executive-committee seat, and do not merge two roles into one person because it would make
a tidier section. If one person genuinely holds two of the three roles (common in a mid-market
business), say so and keep all three sections, since the agendas still differ.

## What counts as evidence

Each bullet must rest on at least one of these, with a ledger Claim ID behind it:

| Evidence | Example |
|---|---|
| **Their own dated words** | A remark on the FY25 earnings call, a conference panel, a trade-press interview, a published post |
| **A commitment the company has made that lands on that desk** | A capex programme, a network target with a date, a lease event, an automation contract, a reporting obligation, a margin commitment |
| **A structural fact of the estate or network** | A lease expiry profile, a site count, a rates revaluation, an EPC deadline, a single-site dependency |
| **A recruitment signal** | An open role and what its job description says the team is being built to do |

**At most one of the three bullets per section may be a role-structural inference** with no direct
evidence, and it must be labelled `*(inference)*` in the text. `gate_runner.py depth` fails a section
carrying two or more. A role-structural inference is a thing that is unavoidably true for anyone in
that seat given the facts established elsewhere in the brief: an eleven-site estate exiting four
sites has a dilapidations exposure whether or not anyone has mentioned it.

## How to write them

- **Bold lead-in naming the agenda item**, then one or two sentences: the evidence with its date,
  then the implication for the conversation. Required by the depth gate.
- Write the fact, then draw the inference. Not "she is worried about Daventry" but "the Daventry
  lease expires in March 2027 and no replacement has been announced, so the decision window is
  already open".
- Keep the title's word "likely" honest. These are informed reads, not claims about someone's inner
  life. Never attribute an emotion, a motive or a private view to a named person.
- One of the three bullets should be something the room would not have guessed. Three obvious
  bullets is a wasted section.
- No CBRE pitch inside these sections. The wedge is the next section. Here you are only describing
  the other side of the table.

## What belongs in each

**Head of Real Estate or Property Director.** The estate as an asset and a liability: lease events
and the expiry profile, the exit and dilapidations exposure, business rates and the revaluation,
capital versus operating treatment, fit-out and capex approval, EPC and MEES deadlines, planning and
consent risk on anything being built, sale-and-leaseback or disposal pressure on the balance sheet,
and being handed a network decision made by somebody else with a date already attached.

**Head of Supply Chain or Logistics Director.** Service and cost: the service promise and what it
costs to keep, inventory levels and working capital, network design and where the pinch points are,
automation business cases and payback, labour availability, cost and industrial relations, carrier
and transport rates, customs and rules-of-origin friction, peak capacity, and the resilience of a
network that has just been told to do more with fewer sites.

**CEO.** The promises made to the market: the earnings and margin commitment, the guidance already
given, capital allocation between growth and deleveraging, integration delivery after a deal, the
growth story and where the next revenue comes from, investor and lender pressure, reporting and
disclosure obligations, and whichever of the above two agendas has become a board-level risk.

## The test each bullet has to pass

Would a broker who read only this section be able to open the meeting with a question that lands,
and would the person across the table recognise their own agenda in it? If not, it is generic, and
generic is worse than absent: it tells the reader the brief was written without research.
